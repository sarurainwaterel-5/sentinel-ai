## Selected policy vs implemented test convention

Owner times use America/Chicago; source market timestamps may use America/New_York.
The isolated interpreter uses Intl timezone conversion with captured Node/ICU/tz
versions. It does not use fixed UTC−6/−5 offsets for policy decisions. Fixture
constructors explicitly target known November instants; seasonal tests transform
them and re-evaluate actual policy boundaries. These fixtures are not an exchange
schedule or a calendar generator.

UTC input convention: exact millisecond canonical `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:mm:ss.sssZ`, finite
valid date/instant, chronological event≤availability≤decision. New York input helper
requires an explicit offset and validates that the local clock matches New York at
that instant. Naive, nonexistent or offset-mismatched times reject. Ambiguous fall
hours require an offset, producing distinct UTC instants. Comparison retains
milliseconds even though displayed wall-clock parts include seconds.

## Exchange-day specification and fixture behavior

Approved calendar must be versioned, product/exchange scoped, effective-dated, with
UTC session intervals, trading-day identity, source provenance, holiday/early-close/
rollover/DST coverage and information availability. One local date is not one exchange
day by assumption. Preserve independent account restriction horizons and their own
identifiers. Noon reopening cannot reset either restriction.

Tests supply a `SYNTHETIC_FIXTURE` calendar with explicit interval and fictional trading
-day ID. Coverage labels TEST_COVERED mean known test assertions only; they do not
mean official coverage or reviewed exchange approval. Interval overlap, missing
coverage, late/expired calendar and missing matching day block new-entry inference.
Synthetic account count must match that exact calendar/version/day; stale, missing,
negative, malformed or mismatched count cannot be assumed zero. No account data is
authenticated by this helper, including when the supplied count is0.

Rollover testing uses an explicit interval crossing a calendar date and preserves its
fictional exchange-day ID/count. This tests interval identity, not the actual CME
rollover time, holiday rules or strategy allowance-consumption event. Calendar inputs
cannot be generated by adding24 hours or by relabeling NY/Chicago midnight.

## Uncertainty and unsupported operational coverage

No official holiday/early-close/exchange calendar was fetched or verified. Unknown
holiday, early-close or rollover coverage blocks new entries in fixtures. Earliest
exchange/account close constraints must eventually be independently enforced before
any operational use; an owner15:00 deadline cannot guarantee the market is available
until15:00. Determining flatten behavior when an actual venue closes earlier requires
verified calendar/operational controls, not an invented schedule. Existing live risk
controls were not changed or audited end-to-end.

Mandatory owner15:00 flatten is still observable as a condition without new-entry
calendar permission; an unknown calendar does not grant entry or postpone that
condition. The helper sends no order and does not establish feasibility of a live
flatten. Position status and target/stop events are synthetic assertions, not fills.

## Replay and compatibility

Pin exact policy, input calendar/version and timezone runtime dependencies. Replay
uses archived calendar data and original cutoff even if current calendar input is
changed. Missing historical dependencies return unavailable; never silently apply a
new calendar to an old result. Old Phase3 NY-date checks and Trading research session
schedules remain unchanged; neither is promoted to verified exchange-day compliance.
